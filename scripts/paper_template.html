Abstract. The resistance-nodulation-division transporter MexB is the inner-membrane component of the principal multidrug efflux pump of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Here we analyse two independent substrate-bound cryo-EM reconstructions of the MexB homotrimer — one with ampicillin bound, one with three molecules of the detergent n-dodecyl-β-D-maltoside (DDM) — using a single reproducible geometric pipeline. Both reconstructions resolve the same asymmetric trimer, with protomers in the access, binding and extrusion states; the binding protomer is indistinguishable between the two maps (§X_E§ Å Cα RMSD), establishing that the conformational description replicates. Ampicillin spans the distal and proximal binding pockets rather than occupying either, making §AMP_NRES§ residue contacts and §AMP_HB§ candidate hydrogen bonds. Atom-composition scoring places the hydrophobic character firmly in the distal pocket (§DBP_APOLAR§% apolar side-chain atoms, mean Kyte-Doolittle §DBP_KD§, eight aromatics) against a markedly more polar proximal pocket (§PBP_APOLAR§%, §PBP_KD§, no aromatics), reproducing the canonical AcrB architecture. Tunnel analysis on the trimer, cross-validated against CAVER 3.0.3, shows the substrate site draining through the PC1/PC2 periplasmic cleft with a bottleneck radius of §AMP_BN§ Å, narrowing to §AMP_BN_W§ Å when ampicillin is left in place. The three DDM molecules occlude §DDM_PCT§% of the binding-protomer site volume, an occupancy observation that we keep explicitly separate from the protein-geometry conclusions.

Introduction

Multidrug efflux pumps of the resistance-nodulation-division (RND) superfamily are the most clinically significant efflux systems in Gram-negative bacteria1. They span the double membrane as a tripartite assembly of an inner-membrane RND transporter, a periplasmic adaptor and an outer-membrane channel, and are energised by the proton motive force. In Pseudomonas aeruginosa, the MexAB-OprM system is the principal constitutively expressed pump, with MexB as its inner-membrane transporter.

The mechanistic framework for this family comes from Escherichia coli AcrB, in which the homotrimer cycles through three states — access, binding and extrusion — by functional rotation. Substrates enter the porter domain, pass from a proximal binding pocket (PBP) to a distal binding pocket (DBP) across a switch loop, and are then expelled towards the exit. The same architecture is annotated in MexB, and the analysis protocol underlying this work was calibrated against the recent MdtF study1, which applied subdomain centroid separations and PC1–PC2 pseudo-contact counts as state diagnostics.

Here we ask three narrow, geometric questions of two substrate-bound MexB reconstructions. Do the two independent maps describe the same conformational states? Where does a bound β-lactam actually sit relative to the two pockets? And what is the geometry of the route from the substrate site to bulk solvent, with and without the bound ligand in place? We deliberately restrict the claims to what protein geometry supports, and treat the detergent-bound structure as an occupancy observation rather than evidence of substrate recognition.

Results

Two independent reconstructions resolve the same asymmetric trimer

Both models comprise three chains (D, E, F) numbered identically to UniProt P52002 with zero offset and no sequence mismatches (Table 1). Chain D is complete over residues 1–1032; chain E spans 1–1030 with gaps at 229 and 360; chain F spans 1–1033 with a gap at 663. The models are therefore directly comparable residue by residue.

Assigning conformational states from PN1–PN2 and PC1–PC2 subdomain centroid separations places chain E in the binding state in both structures (§AMP_E_PN§/§AMP_E_PC§ Å and §DDM_E_PN§/§DDM_E_PC§ Å) and chain F in the extrusion state (§AMP_F_PN§/§AMP_F_PC§ Å and §DDM_F_PN§/§DDM_F_PC§ Å; Fig. 1, Table 2). Chain D of the DDM structure is cleanly access (§DDM_D_PN§/§DDM_D_PC§ Å). Chain D of the ampicillin structure is the one genuinely ambiguous protomer: its PN1–PN2 separation of §AMP_D_PN§ Å reads as access while its PC1–PC2 separation of §AMP_D_PC§ Å reads as binding. We report this as a conflict rather than forcing an assignment.

Comparing the same protomer between the two reconstructions, chain E differs by only §X_E§ Å over all Cα atoms and §X_E_SW§ Å across the switch loop — below the threshold at which reconstruction-to-reconstruction noise can be distinguished from real conformational change. The binding protomer is therefore the same structure in both maps, and every observation made on it replicates. Chains D and F differ by §X_D§ and §X_F§ Å respectively and are not the same state between the two models (Fig. 7).

Ampicillin spans both binding pockets

Ampicillin is modelled in the binding protomer (chain E) of the first structure. Its centroid lies §AMP_DBP§ Å from the distal-pocket centroid and §AMP_PBP§ Å from the proximal-pocket centroid — that is, between the two rather than within either. A per-atom assignment splits the molecule across both pockets, so we describe it as spanning them.

The ligand makes contacts with §AMP_NRES§ residues within 4.5 Å and §AMP_HB§ candidate hydrogen bonds. It is anchored by a salt bridge from K151 to both carboxylate oxygens (2.81 and 2.84 Å) and packs against an aromatic cluster of F178 (3.25 Å, 37 atomic contacts, 25 of them to ring atoms), F615 (3.35 Å) and F610 (3.76 Å). No heavy-atom pair lies below 2.6 Å, so the pose carries no steric strain. Contacts to proximal residues (K151, Q176) and distal residues (F178, F610, V612, F615) occur simultaneously, which is the structural basis for the spanning assignment.

Across all four modelled ligands, §N_MATRIX§ residues make at least one contact and §N_SHARED§ are contacted by more than one ligand (Fig. 4). The shared set is dominated by switch-loop and distal-pocket aromatics, consistent with a common recognition surface, but we note that three of the four ligands are detergent and this matrix should not be read as a substrate-specificity fingerprint.

The distal pocket is the hydrophobic one

Atom-composition scoring of the two pockets, computed independently in all six protomers, gives the distal pocket §DBP_APOLAR§% apolar side-chain atoms with a mean Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy of §DBP_KD§ and eight aromatic residues, against §PBP_APOLAR§% apolar, §PBP_KD§ and no aromatic residues for the proximal pocket (Fig. 5). The ordering is identical in every protomer of both structures. This reproduces the canonical AcrB picture of a hydrophobic trap at the distal site and settles, in favour of the atom-composition result, an earlier reading from surface renderings that had suggested the reverse.

The two pockets form one continuous cavity

Unguided cavity detection on the ligand-stripped trimer finds, in the binding protomer of both structures, a single cavity that overlaps 13 of 14 distal-pocket residues and 14 of 17 proximal-pocket residues (Table 5). The proximal and distal sites are therefore not separate compartments in these models but two ends of one continuous volume, which is the geometry a proximal-to-distal transfer step requires. The same is true of the access protomer in both structures. In the extrusion protomer of the DDM structure the detection is unreliable — the best distal-overlapping cavity is only 54 Å3 — and we do not interpret it.

The substrate site drains through the PC1/PC2 periplasmic cleft

Tunnels were traced on the intact trimer — never on an isolated protomer, whose artificially open protomer–protomer interface admits spurious escape routes — seeded on the bound ligand where present and on the transferred site position otherwise. In the ligand-free binding protomer of the ampicillin structure, the widest route from the substrate site to bulk solvent has a bottleneck radius of §AMP_BN§ Å and exits through the PC1/PC2 periplasmic cleft. The constriction is lined by §AMP_CONS§ (residue:clearance in Å). The equivalent route in the DDM structure has a bottleneck of §DDM_BN§ Å through the same cleft, so the exit geometry replicates across the two maps.

These values were cross-checked against CAVER 3.0.32 run on the same trimers from the same seed points. §CAVER_AGREE§ of §CAVER_N§ ligand-free comparisons agree to within 0.05 Å, and CAVER independently identifies the same constriction-lining residues in the same order (Table 4). CAVER could not be used to check the ligand-in-place condition, because it discards most of an arbitrary ligand’s atoms (Methods). The two implementations differ in reported path length because they terminate the path by different criteria, so only the bottleneck radii are directly comparable.

Density validation revises three of the model-quality flags

All of the above rests on coordinates. Scoring both models against their own cryoSPARC volumes (half A, half B and sharpened; 2.19 Å and 2.04 Å) changes three conclusions and confirms a fourth. Every finding below replicates across both independent half maps (|ΔRSCC| < 0.07 for every flagged group, typically < 0.03) and is quoted as percentile rank within a single map, because raw correlation is not comparable between datasets differing in voxel size, sharpening and masking.

R971 is correctly modelled. Its 12–17 Å separation from D407 and D408 had been assumed to be a side-chain rotamer error. It is not: R971 sits at or above its map's median in every well-resolved protomer (chain E of the ampicillin map 0.570 against a median of 0.533; chains E and F of the DDM map 0.714 and 0.631 against 0.572). The separation is a real feature of these structures and needs a mechanistic explanation rather than a rebuild.

Chain F of the ampicillin reconstruction is not supported by its own density. Whole-protomer median RSCC is 0.182, the 21st percentile of that map, against 0.578 for chain D and 0.608 for chain E; the relay residues score negative (THR976F −0.164, LYS939F −0.081, ARG971F −0.004), where the same residues reach 0.63–0.70 in the DDM map. This is an internal, same-map comparison, so no cross-dataset caveat applies. Chain F relay distances and state assignment from this structure are therefore not determinable, and are excluded. This also accounts for the previously unexplained discrepancy in chain F relay geometry between the two models.

The ampicillin pose is only weakly supported. The ligand scores at the 26th–34th percentile of its map, inside a chain that is otherwise well resolved — so this reflects the ligand, not its environment. The contact distances reported above should be treated as provisional pending a rebuild.

All three DDM molecules are real, but not equally. LMT2001 and LMT2002 sit at the 40th–52nd percentile and LMT2003 at the 14th–21st. The §DDM_PCT§% occlusion figure is therefore not resting on a phantom, but LMT2003 is the weakest feature it depends on.

One artefact deserves recording so it is not mistaken for a modelling error in future work: D407 and D408 score below baseline in most protomers of both maps while K939, R971 and T976 do not. Acidic side chains are preferentially decarboxylated under electron irradiation8,9, so a weak aspartate is expected at these doses.

The energy barrier does not coincide with the geometric bottleneck

Geometry says where a tunnel is narrow; it does not say where a particular ligand finds the passage hard. To separate the two we pulled ampicillin along the chain E route with CaverDock 1.27, which docks the ligand at successive discs and returns a binding-energy profile. The barrier is §CD_BARRIER§ kcal/mol, peaking §CD_EPOS§ Å along the path where the tunnel is §CD_ERAD§ Å wide. The geometric constriction lies a further §CD_GAP§ Å along, at §CD_RPOS§ Å and §CD_RRAD§ Å, by which point the energy has relaxed again (Fig. 8). Radius alone would therefore have identified the wrong residues as rate-limiting for this substrate.

This result is provisional in a way the geometric results are not. Only the lower bound converged — the upper-bound calculation, which additionally enforces a continuous trajectory, did not — so the true barrier is at least this large and probably larger. It was computed at the lowest exhaustiveness setting on a rigid receptor. We report it because the qualitative separation between the energetic and geometric bottlenecks is large and unambiguous, not because the number itself is converged.

The switch-loop gate is a local widening, not a through-route

Because the switch loop is mechanistically central, we measured the F615 gate directly (Fig. 6, Table 6). In every protomer of both structures the gate region is locally roomy — 3.1 to 4.6 Å of clearance — yet a path forced to pass through it bottlenecks at 0.5 to 1.2 Å. The switch-loop region in these models is therefore a widening adjacent to the transfer path rather than the narrowest point of the exit route. The gate is widest in the two binding protomers (4.18 and 4.60 Å) and narrower in the access and extrusion protomers, the same ordering in both reconstructions.

Bound ligand occludes the site and narrows the exit route

Recomputing the site volume with each structure's own ligands in place quantifies occlusion, but the two ligands must be quoted differently. Ampicillin displaces a constant 387 Å3 at every sphere radius tested (14–20 Å), while the percentage this represents runs from 27% down to 12% as the sphere grows, because the denominator grows and the ligand does not; the volume is the robust quantity. The three DDM molecules occupy §DDM_PCT§% of the equivalent volume (§DDM_VFREE§ → §DDM_VOCC§ Å3), essentially filling it (Fig. 3, Table 7). Here the percentage is the robust quantity — it holds at 97.8–98.8% across the same sphere range, because three molecules saturate the pocket and the free volume collapses to a constant 36 Å3 however widely it is measured. Site volumes in the access and extrusion protomers are unchanged, as expected since no ligand is modelled there.

The bound ligand also narrows the exit route: the widest path from the site falls from §AMP_BN§ to §AMP_BN_W§ Å with ampicillin in place, and from §DDM_BN§ to §DDM_BN_W§ Å with the DDM molecules in place. In both cases the widest remaining route switches channel, away from the PC1/PC2 cleft. We regard the ampicillin number as the meaningful one; the DDM figure describes what a detergent at purification concentration does to the geometry, not what a substrate does.

The pocket itself is the same size in both models

Because the detergent structure might be suspected of a distorted or enlarged pocket, we measured the ligand-free site volume of the binding protomer in both models in a single common frame, superposing on the pocket-lining residues (39 Cα, fit RMSD 0.60 Å) so that the measuring sphere sits identically in both. At the default 16 Å radius the DDM model appears 174 Å3 (9.4%) larger, but that difference is not a property of the pocket: it falls monotonically with sphere radius, reaching +0.1% at 18 Å and reversing to −4.3% at 20 Å. Across 27 combinations of grid step, probe radius and sphere radius the parameter-induced spread in the absolute volume is 13.5 times the between-structure difference, and pyKVFinder — an independent method with no arbitrary sphere — puts the difference in the opposite direction (5632 against 5341 Å3). We therefore find no evidence that the substrate pocket differs in size between the two reconstructions, which is what the 0.86 Å whole-protomer agreement already implied.

The pocket does not enlarge for larger ligands

The two reconstructions agree with each other, but agreement between two models of the same protein is a weak test of whether the substrate pocket responds to what is bound in it. We therefore extended the common-frame measurement to every MexB-containing entry in the Protein Data Bank. A full-text search returns §N_ENTRIES§ entries; 6TA5 contains no MexB chain and the engineered MexB–MexY chimeras 22XK and 22XM fail a residue-identity check at the pocket positions, being roughly 40% identical to MexB, leaving §N_STRUCT§ structures and §N_PROT§ protomers. Each protomer was superposed on the §N_LINING§ pocket-lining Cα of a single reference and the free volume measured with a sphere fixed in that reference frame, so the sphere occupies the same anatomical position throughout.

Across the §N_BOUND§ ligand-bound protomers the bound ligands span a §LIG_RANGE§-fold range of size, from chloramphenicol at §LIG_MIN§ heavy atoms to three DDM molecules at §LIG_MAX§. Pocket volume is uncorrelated with that range (Pearson r = §R_SIZE§ at a 16 Å sphere; Fig. 9). The two independent structures that contain the identical ligand, 2V50 and 3W9I, differ by §NOISE§ Å3 — more than any difference attributable to ligand size, and a fair estimate of the method's noise floor. Volume is likewise uncorrelated with how far into the porter domain the ligand sits (r = §R_DEPTH§).

Pocket size is set by the conformational state, not the cargo

The same measurement is not insensitive: applied to all §N_PROT§ protomers it separates the three conformational states cleanly (Fig. 10). Free volume is §V_BIND§ Å3 in binding protomers, §V_EXT§ in extrusion and §V_ACC§ in access, a §V_RATIO§-fold range between binding and access, and the ordering holds within every structure taken individually. All six protomers of our two reconstructions fall inside the published spread for their state, the largest departure being §WORST_LAB§ at §WORST_Z§ standard deviations.

Two volume measures are reported because the first degenerates. A seed-based connected volume requires a free grid point at the sphere centre; the midpoint of the two pocket centroids lies inside an atom in most protomers, and the fallback to the nearest free voxel lands in an isolated cavity in §N_BUBBLE§ of §N_PROT§ protomers, returning a fraction of an Å3 that rounds to a plausible-looking zero. Those rows are flagged rather than reported. Total free volume within the sphere requires no seed, is defined for every protomer, and is what Fig. 10 shows; the two measures give the same ordering.

The lining does not rearrange either

A pocket can accept a different ligand by rotating its side chains at constant volume, so the χ1 angles of the pocket aromatics were measured in every protomer (Fig. 12). §W_ROT_OK§ of the §W_ROT§ aromatics in the multi-ligand protomer sit within §ROT_MAX§° of the published binding-protomer mean: three ligands at once do not rotate the lining. The exception, §ROT_RES§, is §F664_DEV§° away, but the ampicillin protomer departs by a similar amount in the same direction, so it tracks these two models rather than the ligand count and should not be quoted before a density check. §ROT_RES§ is otherwise strictly state-coupled across the published set. Residue 626 is a methionine in MexB, so the F626 that is central to the MdtF hydrophobic nook has no counterpart here.

The aromatic core is family scaffold; the outer rim is not

If the pocket is pre-organised rather than induced, what the stations are built from becomes the question. Aligning MexB to seven RND transporters (Fig. 13) shows the pocket aromatics to be §C_AROM§% identical across the panel against §C_OTHER§% for everything else lining the pocket, with Y327 and F628 invariant in all eight. MexY and AcrD, the two transporters in the panel that prefer aminoglycosides — polar, cationic substrates that MexB exports poorly — are the two that lose the aromatic core, retaining §C_MEXY§ and §C_ACRD§ of MexB's ten pocket aromatics as aromatic against §C_BROAD§ for the five broad-spectrum transporters.

Conservation across the three stations falls as they approach the entrance: §C_DEEP§% at the deepest, §C_MID§% at the middle and §C_OUT§% at the outermost, whose F573 is identical in none of the seven. The deep site is therefore family scaffold and the outer stations are transporter-specific, which is a mechanism for polyspecificity rather than a restatement of it.

The site changes chemistry around the cycle

Volume is only one descriptor. The lipophilic index of the site surface — the fraction of its lining atoms that are carbon or sulfur — is §L_BIND§% in binding protomers, §L_ACC§% in access and §L_EXT§% in extrusion, so the site is at its most hydrophobic in the state that binds substrate and its least in the state that releases it. Poisson–Boltzmann potential over the same sphere is negative in all §N_APBS§ protomers computed, without a single exception, and most negative in access (§E_ACC§ kT/e) against §E_BIND§ in binding and §E_EXT§ in extrusion (Fig. 14). An electronegative, less hydrophobic mouth handing to a hydrophobic binding site is consistent with the cationic character of many RND substrates, although the within-state spread is wide enough that only the ordering should be read.

The proton relay is intact except for R971

Side-chain functional-atom distances across the transmembrane proton relay are consistent with an engaged network at D407/D408 and K939 (Table 8). In the ampicillin structure, chain D gives D407–K939 2.71 Å and D408–K939 2.70 Å. R971, however, lies 12–17 Å from both aspartates in all six protomers of both structures. Because R971 is essential by mutagenesis and acts as the electrostatic switch in the AcrB relay, a separation of this size is far more likely to be a side-chain rotamer problem in the models than a biological observation, and it does not change between the two reconstructions. We flag it as an unresolved modelling issue and draw no mechanistic conclusion from it.

Discussion

The central strength of this analysis is replication. Two independently determined reconstructions give a binding protomer that differs by §X_E§ Å overall and §X_E_SW§ Å across the switch loop, and every geometric conclusion drawn on that protomer — the state assignment, the pocket hydropathy ordering, the continuity of the two pockets, the identity and width of the exit route — is the same in both. Results that hold across two maps are considerably stronger than results from one, and we have marked throughout which observations do so.

Ampicillin spanning both pockets rather than occupying either is consistent with the transfer step the AcrB framework requires, and the simultaneous K151 salt bridge and F178 aromatic packing gives a plausible structural mechanism for holding a substrate across the switch loop. We would caution against over-reading a single pose: the protocol underlying this work records that an earlier model version of this same ligand had a different contact set and a steric interpenetration with F610, so contact lists must be regenerated whenever the model is rebuilt, and were regenerated here.

The strongest caution concerns the detergent structure. DDM at purification concentration will occupy any groove that fits it, and the three modelled molecules do exactly that, filling §DDM_PCT§% of the binding-protomer site. That they contact distal-pocket and switch-loop residues is a statement about the shape of the groove, not about substrate recognition, and we have kept the protein-geometry arguments in this paper separate from the detergent-occupancy arguments throughout. The contact matrix in Fig. 4 in particular should not be read as a specificity fingerprint when three of its four columns are detergent.

Two further limitations deserve statement. First, the proximal-pocket residue set used here was assembled from AcrB literature rather than from a MexB-specific source, so any conclusion that depends sensitively on proximal-pocket membership — the pocket assignment of a ligand, the proximal hydropathy value — inherits that uncertainty. Second, the R971 geometry is almost certainly a modelling artefact and should be resolved against density before the proton relay in these models is used for any mechanistic argument. Neither issue affects the tunnel or volume results.

Finally, on the tunnel geometry itself: the pipeline used here and CAVER 3.0.3 are independent implementations of different algorithms, and they agree on the ligand-free bottleneck radii to within 0.05 Å and on the identity of the constriction-lining residues. We therefore regard the exit-route description as well supported. The switch-loop result — that F615 marks a local widening rather than the constriction of the exit route — runs against the expectation encoded in the analysis protocol, and is stated here as what two independent tunnel calculations find.

Methods

Structures analysed

Two coordinate models of the P. aeruginosa MexB homotrimer were analysed: an ampicillin-bound model (ligand ZZ7, one copy in chain E) and a DDM-bound model (ligand LMT, three copies in chain E). Structure determination is not described here; only coordinate models were available to this analysis. Ligand B-factors in both models are group-refined — a single value per molecule — and carry no per-atom information.

Sequence and numbering validation

Each chain was translated and compared residue by residue against UniProt P520023 at zero offset; an offset scan over ±5 residues confirmed that zero is optimal. Chains, residue ranges, gaps, HETATM records and alternate locations were enumerated. The pipeline halts on any mismatch or non-zero offset.

Conformational state assignment

Porter subdomains were defined as PN1 (40–130), PN2 (136–180, 278–281, 284–328), PC1 (571–666) and PC2 (679–717, 814–818, 821–858). For each protomer we computed PN1–PN2 and PC1–PC2 Cα centroid separations and pseudo-contact counts (Cα–Cα pairs within 10 Å), and assigned the nearest of the published access, binding and extrusion reference states by L1 distance. Where the two diagnostics select different states the conflict is reported rather than resolved.

Superposition and inter-protomer analysis

Superpositions used the Kabsch algorithm on Cα atoms under two frames: a trimmed transmembrane set (10–35, 337–495, 516–565, 876–1030), which omits the mobile 496–515 cytoplasmic loop and the 859–875 junction, and the porter domain. Whole-protomer RMSD between structures is reported from an all-Cα best fit. Per-residue deviations were smoothed with a 9-residue sliding window and exported as ChimeraX .defattr attributes and as B-factor-painted coordinates.

Ligand environment

Contacts were enumerated at 4.5 Å between ligand and protein heavy atoms, with minimum distance, contact count and aromatic-ring contact count per residue. Candidate hydrogen bonds were taken as N/O to N/O pairs within 3.6 Å. Any heavy-atom pair below 2.6 Å is flagged; such pairs are additionally classified as a short hydrogen bond when both partners are N or O, which distinguishes them from steric overlap. Pocket assignment used distance from the ligand centroid to the distal and proximal pocket centroids together with a per-atom nearest-pocket call, so that a molecule spanning both is described as spanning rather than forced into one.

Cavity and pocket analysis

Cavity detection used pyKVFinder4 (step 0.6 Å, probe_in 1.4 Å, probe_out 4.0 Å, removal distance 2.4 Å), both ligand-guided and unguided, on the trimer with ligands stripped. The cavity containing the substrate site was identified by distal-pocket residue overlap. fpocket5 was run on the same ligand-stripped trimers. Pocket hydrophobicity was scored by side-chain atom composition (carbon against N/O/S, backbone excluded) and by mean Kyte-Doolittle hydropathy6.

Site volumes are grid-based: free space within 16 Å of the midpoint of the distal and proximal pocket centroids, connected to that point, on a 0.5 Å grid with a 1.4 Å probe. They were computed with the structure's own ligands stripped, so that sites are comparable across structures, and again with ligands present, to quantify occlusion. These volumes are internally comparable across these two structures only and are not drop-in replacements for fpocket or CASTp values.

Tunnel calculation

Tunnels were computed on the intact trimer. A regular 0.6 Å grid was built over the whole assembly and each voxel assigned a clearance, the distance to the nearest atomic van der Waals surface. Bulk solvent was defined as the connected region of clearance ≥ 3.0 Å reaching the edge of the box. The bottleneck is the maximum over all paths of the minimum clearance, found exactly by a widest-path (max-min) Dijkstra search from the seed; the path is then centred on the local medial axis by constrained perpendicular optimisation and resampled at 0.15 Å, which removes the grid-discretisation bias that otherwise under-reports narrow gaps unevenly. Successive distinct tunnels were found by blocking the constriction of the previous one. Seeds were the ligand centroid where the chain carries a ligand and the distal/proximal pocket midpoint otherwise. Each calculation was run twice, once with all ligands stripped and once with them present as obstructions; the difference quantifies occlusion of the exit route. Paths are exported as pseudo-atom traces with the local radius in the B-factor column.

Cross-validation against CAVER

CAVER 3.0.32 was run on the same trimers, seeded at the same coordinates, with probe_radius 0.9, shell_radius 3 and shell_depth 4. Bottleneck radii and bottleneck-lining residues were compared against the pipeline's own output. Note on ligand handling: CAVER assigns atomic radii from its own atom table and silently discards atoms it cannot place. For these ligands that means most of the molecule — 8 of ampicillin’s 25 heavy atoms and 78 of DDM’s 105 were loaded — and this is true whether the ligand is written as HETATM or as ATOM. CAVER’s ligand-in-place runs are therefore not a valid cross-check and are excluded from the agreement statistics; the ligand-free comparison is unaffected. The occlusion results in this paper rest on the pipeline implementation alone.

Ligand transport energetics

CaverDock 1.27, which uses a docking engine derived from AutoDock Vina 1.1.2, was used to pull ampicillin along the chain E tunnel. The tunnel centreline computed above was resampled at 0.5 Å into CaverDock’s disc format over the first 32 Å from the substrate site. Receptor and ligand were converted to PDBQT with OpenBabel using Gasteiger charges, the receptor kept rigid; the search box was sized to enclose the tunnel with a 12 Å margin. The calculation was run under MPI at exhaustiveness 1 with multiple_search 3. Only the lower-bound trajectory converged; the upper bound is not reported. Energies are therefore a lower estimate of the true barrier and are described as provisional throughout.

Density validation

Each model was scored against its own half maps and sharpened volume. For every residue and ligand we computed the real-space correlation between the experimental density and density simulated from the model within a 2.2 Å mask, together with per-atom map values expressed in sigma above solvent. Three calibration points matter and were established empirically. Correlation rises monotonically as the assumed resolution approaches the true value, so the real resolutions (2.19 and 2.04 Å) were used rather than a default. Sharpened cryoSPARC volumes are solvent-masked, which shrinks the whole-box sigma several-fold relative to their own unmasked half maps, so z-scores are referenced to a solvent shell around the model instead of the box. Absolute correlation is not comparable between the two datasets, which differ in voxel size (0.65 against 0.83 Å), sharpening and masking; all findings are therefore stated as percentile rank within a single map. On like-for-like sharpened maps the two models fit comparably (median protein RSCC 0.533 and 0.572).

Resolutions were confirmed independently from the half-map pairs by Fourier shell correlation. The supported / marginal / weak verdicts use a threshold of 0.7 times the map median, a working heuristic rather than a community standard.

Statistics and reproducibility

The pipeline is deterministic; no stochastic method is used and no seeds affect the reported values. Everything under results/ regenerates from a clean working directory with a single command. §NPASS§ of §NCHECK§ automated checks against the reference values encoded in the analysis protocol pass on the run that produced this manuscript; the full check list is written to results/tables/validation.csv on every run.
